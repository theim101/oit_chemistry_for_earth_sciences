## Introduction

This week we will learn how to use the principles of redox chemistry to draw Pourbaix diagrams, also known as Eh-pH or pE-pH diagrams. These are very useful to graphically represent the stable phases (in the liquid or solid) at a certain combination of redox potential and pH. These types of diagrams, also referred to as stability diagrams, are commonly used in geochemistry.

For this, we first need to consider the practical use of E<sub>h</sub> a bit more in detail. While E<sub>h</sub> might seem similar to pH, in reality it is not. Both can be measured with a probe, but while pH is a direct measure of the proton concentration, E<sub>h</sub> does not measure electron concentration directly, since electrons don't exist freely in solution. Rather, it is a measure of the 'tendency of a solution to donate or accept electrons'. To understand this better, look at how a pH probe and an ORP (Oxidation Reduction Potential) probe or sensor work. You can also have a look on websites of sensor manufacturers, many of them have simple explanations of how their products work. See for example [the explanation from Hamilton:](https://www.hamiltoncompany.com/knowledge-base/article/orp-basics?srsltid=AfmBOoqQh-vzIYEy7bIrraa7PyhW9Oej7Nh_xMBcRpqSNvbqr26-7nBo).
