## Introduction

This week we will study electrochemistry: redox. We will use chapter 20 from **Tro, Chemistry: A molecular approach**, and several paragraphs in [Microbiology for Earth Scientists](https://newprairiepress.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1053&context=ebooks). We will get back to this book later in the course. Just as in the previous 3 weeks, you will mostly work through the material yourself, with an instructor available during most of the scheduled self-study and exercise classes. The lectures on Monday will walk you through the main concepts of the chapter, and give you the opportunity to ask clarifying questions based on your pre-reading of the material. Then you will go to the exercises given at the end of the chapter. More details on this below. On Thursday, any remaining material will be briefly discussed if needed, and if any questions stood out during the practice hours on Monday those will be addressed as well. To complete your learnings of the week, you will test your progress with a set of problems integrating the different concepts you learned. This will also give you an indication of what is expected of you in the exam.

This week will help prepare you for week 8, where we use the principles of redox chemistry to draw Pourbaix diagrams, also known as E<sub>H</sub>-pH or pe-pH diagrams. These are very useful to graphically represent the stable phases (in the liquid or solid) at a certain combination of redox potential and pH. These types of diagrams, also referred to as stability diagrams, are commonly used in geochemistry.

-   **20.4** Electrode and cell potentials: how does the overall cell potential (E<sup>0</sup><sub>cell</sub>) relate to the potentials of individual electrodes? Try worked examples 20.4 and 20.5 and then do **exercises** **45, 47, 53, 55, 61**.

-   **20.5** How are E<sup>0</sup><sub>cell</sub>, 𝚫G<sup>0</sup> and the equilibrium constant (K) related? If needed, go back to Chapter 19 (19.8 - 19.10). Go through worked example 20.6 and 20.7 and do exercises xxxxxxxxxxxxxxx.

-   **20.6** We now know how to calculate the cell potential at standard conditions (E<sup>0</sup><sub>cell</sub>), but how do we account for non-standard conditions (E<sub>cell</sub>), e.g. different concentrations? For this, we need to use the Nernst equation. Make sure you understand its derivation, explained in the text. Do you also understand the difference between Q and K (check 19.10)? In 20.6 the concept of concentration cells is explained, an electrochemical cell with the same half reactions at both electrodes (fig 20.12). Do you understand how this works? Do **exercises 73, 75, 77, 79, 81**.

-   **20.8** How can we use electricity to drive non-spontaneous reactions forward?
